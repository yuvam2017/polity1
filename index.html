50 Years of National Emergency (1975)
Art. 352–360 ▼
📰 In News: June 25, 1975 — 50th Anniversary
Core Facts
Source
Borrowed from Weimar Constitution, Germany
Articles
352–360, Part XVIII
3 Instances
1962 (China), 1971 (Pakistan), 1975 (Internal)
Authority
President + written decision of Union Cabinet
Parliament Approval
Both Houses within 1 month
Duration
6 months; extended every 6 months by Parliament
Majority
Special Majority = total membership + 2/3rd present & voting
Revocation
President anytime; Lok Sabha by simple majority
FRs during Emergency
Can suspend under Art. 359, EXCEPT Art. 20 & 21
🎯 UPSC Trap Art. 20 (protection against conviction) & Art. 21 (life & liberty) CANNOT be suspended even during National Emergency — 44th Amendment ensured this. Very commonly tested.
Types of Emergency
TypeArticleGrounds
National352War / External aggression / Armed rebellion
President's Rule356Constitutional machinery breakdown / Art. 365
Financial360Threat to financial stability or credit of India
🎯 Trap "Internal disturbance" ground was replaced by "Armed rebellion" through 44th Amendment, 1978. Original Constitution had "internal disturbance."
Effect on Centre–State Relations
Centre → directions on ANY subject Parliament → legislates on State List (Art. 250) President → may modify revenue distribution Legislatures extended 1 yr at a time (not beyond 6 months after emergency ends)
Constitutional Amendments Timeline
1975
38th Amendment — Barred judicial review of Emergency declaration
1975
39th Amendment — Parliament to decide election disputes (President, VP, PM, Speaker)
1976
42nd Amendment — Sweeping changes: Directive Principles primacy, curtailed judicial powers, extended Lok Sabha term to 6 yrs
1977
Shah Commission — Probe into Emergency abuses & excesses
1978
44th Amendment — Replaced "internal disturbance" with "armed rebellion"; restored judicial review; Art. 20 & 21 non-suspendable; LS term back to 5 yrs; Right to Property → Art. 300A
📝 Note: 44th Amendment Key Changes • Cabinet must communicate to President in writing
• Removed Article 257A (deployment of Union forces in States)
• Restored judicial review for President & VP election disputes
• Right to Property: FR → Constitutional Right under Art. 300A

Assent to State Bills
Art. 200/201 ▼
📰 SC 16th Presidential Reference (Art. 143)
SC Ruling: Key Takeaways
Deemed Assent
Unconstitutional — courts can't assume assent merely because time lapsed
Indefinite Delay
Governor/President CANNOT sit on bills indefinitely — violates federalism
Judicial Review
Limited — only if malafide intent; review confined to procedure not merits
SC Advice
President need NOT seek SC advice; subjective satisfaction sufficient
Governor's Options under Art. 200 — 4 Actions
✓ Grant Assent → Bill becomes Law ✗ Withhold Assent → Bill rejected ↩ Return for Reconsideration (not Money Bills) 📋 Reserve for President (Art. 201)
🎯 Trap If legislature re-passes a bill (with/without changes), Governor MUST give assent. Governor cannot reserve a re-enacted bill unless materially altered.
President's Options under Art. 201 — 3 Actions
✓ Grant Assent ✗ Withhold Assent ↩ Return for Reconsideration → Legislature passes within 6 months → President makes final decision
🎯 Key Difference Unlike Governor, President is NOT obligated to give assent to a reconsidered Bill — can still withhold.
Governor's Discretion — When Bound by Council of Ministers?
📝 Note: Art. 163(2) Governor acts in own discretion in specified spheres. Exceptions where Governor has discretion:
HC powers · Art. 288 (taxes on water/electricity) · Art. 360 (Financial Emergency) · Art. 254(2) (Concurrent List) · Art. 304(b) (Interstate trade) · Art. 31A/31C · Unconstitutional Bills
✅ Important • Inaction = Unconstitutional → challengeable via writ of mandamus
• Writ of mandamus CANNOT be issued against: private body · President of India · State Governors · Chief Justice of HC (judicial capacity)
• Art. 361(2): Governor has absolute immunity from criminal proceedings while in office
🎯 Trap Art. 361(1): Governor not answerable to any court for powers/duties. But government's actions remain open to judicial review.

Election Procedure (Art. 66)
Electoral College
Both Houses of Parliament (elected + nominated); NOT state legislatures

Proportional Representation + Single Transferable Vote + Secret Ballot

Each vote = 1 (unlike Presidential election)

20 proposers + 20 seconders; ₹15,000 deposit

🎯 Trap 39th Amendment removed Supreme Court's authority to adjudicate VP election petitions. A body constituted by Parliament resolves these disputes.

Speaker & Deputy Speaker of Lok Sabha
Art. 93–95 ▼
About Office of Speaker (Art. 93)
Election
By Lok Sabha members — simple majority present & voting; date fixed by President
Term
Does NOT vacate until before first meeting of new Lok Sabha after dissolution
Removal (Art. 94)
Majority of ALL THEN members; minimum 14 days' notice
During removal
Cannot preside; CAN speak & vote in first instance (not on equality of votes)
Chairpersons Panel
Speaker nominates up to 10 MPs under Section 9, Rules of Procedure
🎯 Trap Speaker's removal requires majority of "all then members" — not simple majority of present & voting. During removal proceedings, Speaker CAN cast vote in first instance but NOT on a tie.
Core Duties of Speaker
Rights Guardian Regulatory Authority Deadlock Resolver (casting vote) Joint Session Presider Money Bill Authority (Art. 110(4))
About Deputy Speaker
Origin
1921 under Govt. of India Act, 1919; Sachidananda Sinha first held the post
Post-Independence First
M.A. Ayyangar — first elected Deputy Speaker
Convention
Post offered to Opposition party (long-standing convention)
Powers
Same powers as Speaker when presiding; NO appeal against rulings
Resignation
Speaker resigns to DS; DS resigns to Speaker (vice-versa)
📝 Art. 95 DS performs Speaker's duties in case of vacancy, absence, etc.

📰 Karnataka MLA disqualified — illegal mining case (CBI court)
Constitutional Grounds (Art. 102 / 191)
Office of Profit Unsound mind (declared by competent court) Undischarged insolvent Not citizen of India / foreign citizenship acquired Defection (10th Schedule) Disqualified by any law of Parliament
Legal Framework — Criminal Conviction (RPA, 1951)
Section 8(3) RPA
Conviction for offences with imprisonment ≥ 2 years → disqualified
Crimes Covered
Bribery, rape, promoting enmity, untouchability, corruption, disloyalty, etc.
Lily Thomas, 2013
Lawmaker immediately disqualified upon such conviction
Manoj Narula, 2014
Appointing Ministers with criminal background not legally barred; PM advised to avoid
PIL Public Interest Foundation, 2018
Courts cannot add new disqualification grounds

Parliamentary Procedures
Adjournment Sine Die
End of session without fixing next date; pending bills unaffected; most notices lapse; President issues prorogation notification (Art. 85)
Zero Hour
Starts ~12 noon; after Question Hour; NOT formally recognised in parliamentary procedure
Question Hour
First hour of every sitting; 3 types: Starred (oral, asterisk), Unstarred (written), Short Notice (oral, <10 days notice)
Parliament frequency
Must meet at least twice a year; no minimum days required
🎯 Trap Zero Hour is not formally recognized in the Rules of Procedure. Question Hour IS formally recognized. This is a classic MCQ trap.
